# Writing: humour, natural speech and truth

## The standard
The humour in every video must be the best it can be, and everything must feel natural and human (the user's words). Test every line and picture against this list before it goes to the user.

## Humour
- The joke is always on **the situation**, never on customers, staff, a real business or a group of people.
- One real, specific detail beats a general one: "turn left at the goat", "2 chkn?? 7 chps", "the price is ON the picture", "seventeen tomatoes, one is pretending to be an onion".
- Put the funny word last in the line, then stop. Do not explain the joke.
- Escalate in threes and let the third break the pattern ("He found a good morning. He found three more good mornings. He found a video of a dancing uncle.").
- Understate. The narrator and Nomsa stay calm while things fall apart.
- Give characters their own short line when it is funnier in their mouth ("Everybody has the screenshot.").
- End on a callback to the opening problem (the "button").
- Recurring jokes build a channel: the goat, Nomsa's attempts to smile, the nephew, Table 7.
- Write three versions of the hook and the button; keep the best.

## Natural and human
- Contractions, short sentences, pauses where a person pauses ("Honestly?", "Okay.", "Sir.", "Chef.").
- No advert language: not "seamlessly", "all in one place", "boost", "solution", "manage your business". Say what happens: "she scans it, and his shop lives on her phone".
- The product part sounds like the same person who told the joke.
- Read every line aloud at speaking speed. If it sounds written, rewrite it.
- Faces react before the line lands: a blink, a look, an eyebrow.

## Local and true
- Local words people use in English: robots (traffic lights), kota, braai, spaza, shisanyama, "my sister", "chef", "gogo", "month-end". Prices in the local currency (E in Eswatini).
- isiZulu, siSwati, Sesotho or other local-language lines only after a native speaker checks them.
- Venda Khona: free for sellers, no fees, no commission. Show only real features (listings with prices and photos, order button, mark as sold, bookings confirmed in one tap, hours, map, QR code, follow and posts, team members, reports with orders, revenue and most-viewed items, CSV export, catalogue PDF, choosing a look and colours).
- Servo: wording as in `servo-pos/docs/POSTING_PLAN.md` section 3; the restaurant's own app is on Standard and Multi-branch only ("Standard plan and up"); payroll is Multi-branch only; never "works offline"; never "fastest", "cheapest", "#1"; "demo data" on demo screens; no invented numbers.
- Captions: present tense, at most 7 words a line, 2 lines, one `*highlighted*` word.

## Shapes that work
**Episode (45 to 70 s):** hook (3 s) - escalate in threes - news desk ("Breaking news", then "In better news") - the app - any device - the button - end card.
**Original short (12 to 18 s):** the situation in one line - the character's punchline - one product fact said like a person - end card. Examples: "Is this your last price?" / "It's my only price." / "Put your prices in your own shop app."
**Cut-down (14 to 18 s):** the funniest whole lines of an episode plus its end card; set in `cuts.json`.
**Explainer (2 to 4 min):** a question people ask, three steps, one joke per step, real screens, a clear next step.
