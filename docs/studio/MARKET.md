# Southern Africa: market research for the cartoon videos

Researched 17 Nov 2026 for Venda Khona (free shop app and marketplace for people who sell) and Servo POS (restaurant till, kitchen screen, table ordering and the restaurant's own guest app). Numbers are as reported by the sources listed at the end; most platform numbers are **advertising reach, not unique people**, and sources often disagree. Use them for direction, not as claims in posts.

## 1. The short version (what this means for the videos)
1. **WhatsApp is the shop counter, Facebook is the town square.** About 94% of South African internet users are on WhatsApp, and 74% of small businesses there use it to reach customers. Facebook is the largest platform with an ad audience in every country checked, and in Eswatini it is effectively the only big one (40.7% of the population; Instagram 6.1%). So: **WhatsApp Status and Facebook Reels first**, TikTok and YouTube Shorts second, Instagram last (except Namibia, where Instagram reaches 16%).
2. **South Africa is different:** TikTok's adult ad reach (29.1 million) is now above Facebook's (27.9 million) and YouTube's (26.9 million). For South African audiences, TikTok and YouTube Shorts matter as much as Facebook.
3. **Very young audiences:** median age 22.5 in Eswatini and 17.9 in Zambia (28.7 in South Africa). Humour should feel like today's skits, not like a corporate advert.
4. **Data is money.** Prices per gigabyte vary from cheap (Malawi, Eswatini under US$1) to painful (South Africa around R20 per GB). Keep WhatsApp copies small (our 720p files are 3 to 6 MB) and the message understandable with the sound off (captions always on).
5. **Most people are not yet online in the poorer markets** (Malawi 18%, Mozambique 20%, Zambia 33%, Zimbabwe 38% internet penetration). Growth there is fast (social media identities +40% to +50% a year in Zimbabwe and Malawi), but the audience today is mostly urban and mobile-only.
6. **The customers are informal and small.** Eswatini has about 70,000 micro, small and medium businesses, 75% of them one-person businesses and 75% informal. South Africa's informal retail is worth around R190 to R220 billion a year across 100,000 to 200,000 spaza shops, and informal fast food (kota, shisanyama, street food) about R90 billion across 50,000 outlets. These owners are the cast of our cartoons: we show *their* day.
7. **South Africans love funny ads that tell a story.** The most celebrated local advertising (Chicken Licken, Nando's) is absurd, character-led and rooted in everyday life and oral storytelling. That is exactly our format: a small disaster, a deadpan news desk, a callback.
8. **Local skit culture is big and growing**: South African creators such as MDM Sketch Comedy, Thenjiwe, Leon Gumede and Ndile, and in Eswatini Pepe & Shodi, Gogo Slwane and Dr Shakes (who say Facebook is their biggest stage). Our cartoons should sit comfortably next to their content: quick, relatable, a character people recognise.
9. **Old jokes to avoid:** load shedding. Eskom reported more than 500 days without load shedding by October 2026; a load-shedding joke now dates the video.
10. **Shorts can be long now**: YouTube Shorts accept up to three minutes (vertical or square, uploads after 15 Oct 2024), WhatsApp Status 90 seconds (reported 2025), Facebook Reels are no longer capped at 90 s in the app. But the hook still has to land in the first 3 seconds, and 15 to 45 seconds is the sweet spot for reach.

## 3. Who we are talking to
**Venda Khona (sellers):** home bakers, braiders, repair men, spaza and tuck-shop owners, people who sell clothes or crafts on WhatsApp and Facebook. They answer the same questions all day ("price?", "is it still available?", "where are you?"), keep sales in a book, and cannot afford a website. Mostly young, mobile-only, price-sensitive, on WhatsApp all day. The promise that lands: **"your own shop app, free, no fees, no commission"**.

**Servo POS (restaurants):** owners and managers of takeaways, kota and shisanyama spots, cafés and sit-down restaurants. Their pain: lost or unreadable order slips, cash that does not add up, running out of stock at lunch, rosters, not knowing what sells, being unable to step away. South African competitors they know: Yoco (card machines and free POS software, Counter tablet about R4,999, about 2.7% per card payment), iKhokha (Mover card machine about R899, about 2.75%), SumUp, and Lightspeed for bigger operations. Servo's difference is **restaurant-specific** (kitchen screen, table QR ordering, stock, staff) and the **restaurant's own guest app** (Standard plan and up).

## 4. Money and payments
- Mobile money is less established in Southern Africa than in East Africa (about 5 million active accounts in the region by one estimate), but growing: MTN MoMo has 13 million registered customers in South Africa; group-wide MoMo reached 69.5 million monthly active users in 2025. Country wallets people know: MTN MoMo (Eswatini, Zambia), EcoCash (Zimbabwe), Airtel Money (Zambia, Malawi), M-Pesa (Mozambique, Lesotho), Orange Money (Botswana). Mention a payment method in a video only where the app really supports it.
- **Stokvels**: about 11 million South Africans in more than 800,000 savings groups moving about R50 billion a year. A stokvel is a natural cartoon setting (group buying, group catering orders).
- **Calendar of money:** month-end payday (around the 25th in South Africa), social grants at the start of each month (older persons first), "long January" after the festive season, Black Friday and the festive season as the biggest selling period.

## 5. Culture, humour and what to avoid
- **Works:** everyday absurdity, recognisable characters (the gogo, the nephew who "knows computers", the customer who types for three minutes and sends "Hi"), deadpan narration, callbacks, local words people use in English (robots for traffic lights, kota, braai, spaza, "my sister", "chef"), prices in the local currency.
- **Careful:** isiZulu or siSwati lines only when a native speaker checks them (Edge has isiZulu voices, `zu-ZA-ThandoNeural` and `zu-ZA-ThembaNeural`, but none for siSwati, Sesotho, Setswana, Shona or Chichewa). Afrikaans voices exist (`af-ZA`). Portuguese for Mozambique: `pt-PT` voices or the multilingual voices.
- **Avoid:** politics, the monarchy in Eswatini, religion as the butt of a joke, tribal or national stereotypes, xenophobia, gambling, alcohol to young audiences, load shedding (dated), real businesses or real people, and any claim we cannot prove (no "fastest", "cheapest", "#1", no invented numbers or testimonials).
- **Cultural moments to plan around:** Umhlanga reed dance in Eswatini (late August or early September; schools close), Incwala (December/January), Easter, South African Heritage Day (24 September, braai day), independence days (Namibia 21 Mar, Zimbabwe 18 Apr, Eswatini 6 Sep, Botswana 30 Sep, Lesotho 4 Oct, Zambia 24 Oct, Mozambique 25 Jun, Malawi 6 Jul), Black Friday, festive season. Dates to be checked each year.

## 6. Platform rules that shape the edit
| Platform | Length | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| WhatsApp Status | up to 90 s (reported 2025) | Send the 720p copy; first frame must work as a still; captions on |
| Facebook Reels | longer than 90 s allowed in the app since mid-2025 (API still 90 s) | Link in the first comment; the main platform in Eswatini, Botswana, Lesotho, Zambia, Malawi |
| TikTok | 15 to 60 s for new audiences | Huge in South Africa and growing fastest in Mozambique |
| YouTube Shorts | up to 3 min (vertical or square, since 15 Oct 2024) | Shorts over 1 min with a Content ID music claim are blocked: our music is our own, so we are safe |
| YouTube (long) | any | Monetisation (YPP): 1,000 subscribers plus 4,000 watch hours in 12 months or 10 million Shorts views in 90 days; a 500-subscriber fan-funding tier exists; YouTube announced stricter thresholds from February 2027 (check the official help page before planning around them) |
| Instagram Reels | 15 to 90 s | Only Namibia (16%) and South Africa (13%) have a sizeable audience |

All platforms: the first 3 seconds decide; captions for silent viewing; vertical 9:16.

## 7. What we still do not know
- Real performance of our own posts (views, shares, clicks on the tagged links): the most important data, which only the user's dashboards have. Record it in the skill's `feedback.md`.
- Whether Venda Khona and Servo serve customers outside Eswatini today (currency, payments, delivery, support). **Do not run country versions until that is confirmed.**
- WhatsApp use by country outside South Africa (only one Zimbabwe survey found).
