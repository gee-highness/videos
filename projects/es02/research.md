## 4. The law, plain (explainers, not legal advice)
- FACT (M&G, 12 Jan 2023, a community legal educator's opinion piece): South Africa has no dangerous-pets-specific legislation. [MG]
- FACT: civil route is the actio de pauperie: owner liable for harm by a domestic animal acting "contrary to its nature", no negligence needed. Defences include the victim being where they were not entitled to be, provocation by the victim, a third party or another animal, and a third party in control failing to prevent it. [MG] => this is why the trespass claim matters so much.
- FACT: criminal route needs intentional or negligent unlawful conduct; owners of a controlled dog have a duty to act; past convictions cited were culpable homicide, not a dog-specific crime. [MG] The statute behind the police case wording is not named in anything read.
- FACT: municipal by-laws fill gaps (the M&G example is Johannesburg's, not Durban's); NSPCA says enforcement is inconsistent. eThekwini rules: UNVERIFIED. [MG][SAN]
- FACT (Dec 2022): NSPCA (Nazareth Appalsamy) "we do not support the ban". A Sizwe Kupelo Foundation petition had 120,000+ signatures. [SAN] (Different Sizwe: the 2022 case. Do not mix them.)
- FACT (undated, c. 2022-23): Dept of Agriculture said government was "working on new laws" so owners of "power dogs" are accountable. No bill or timeline. [ECR] 2024-26 status UNVERIFIED. Say "there is still no national law written for dangerous dogs, according to the latest legal explainers" or leave the status line out.
- UNVERIFIED: a 2025 court ruling allowing a municipality to impound three dogs as a public nuisance (allAfrica, blocked).
