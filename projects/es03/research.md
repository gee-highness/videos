## 3. The culture (Igba Nkwu = "wine carrying")
- FACT (Wikipedia, edited 25 Nov 2026): a significant cultural ceremony among the Igbo of south-eastern Nigeria that formalises a union. The bride carries a cup of palm wine, with her friends (Umuagbo), to find her groom among the guests; she sips, offers him the rest, and he drinks. "This symbolizes their union." Witnessed by both families and the community.
- FACT (WePost Weddings, 30 Sep 2026): her father hands her the cup and tells her to give it to the man she has chosen; guests try to distract her, men offer their hands, the groom often hides; she kneels and offers him the cup; the search "takes a few minutes at most". CLAIM (WePost): giving the cup is her public acceptance of him.
- CONFLICT: Wikipedia has her sip first; WePost says in many communities she sips after him. Do not state the order.
- FACT (WePost): afterwards the couple dance and guests spray them with money.
- FACT (Wikipedia): dancing is a prominent part of the festivities. Brides and maidens dance before spectators.
- Place in the sequence: the wine carrying is stage 6 of 8, after bride price (Wikipedia); many couples also do a church wedding.
- UNVERIFIED (page blocked): that pouring away a bride's drink means rejecting her (Linda Ikeji's Blog, 2025). Not used.
- Nigerian legal status of igba nkwu: NOT verified. Do not mention in the video.

## 6. Music, DJ and MC at Nigerian weddings (background, vendor-level)
- CLAIM (Event Design By BE, a wedding planner's commercial blog, undated): Igbo weddings can feature dance troupes for the bride's entrance, masquerades with drummers and highlife bands; the MC (compere) can facilitate rites such as the bride's entrance. Does not describe the wine-carrying moment.
- CLAIM (Vanguard via allAfrica, 25 Oct 2026, MC Dammy): "Understanding Owambe culture is a cheat code for an MC." An MC should combine "the traditions, the humour and the music". Not Igbo-specific.
- CLAIM (africa.com, 2018 list): Flavour's "Ada" is "a traditional wedding anthem". One old editorial list, not a documented tradition. Not used.
- CLAIM (vendor advice: The Knot, Naija Event Experts, ikoniconcepts, Pulse Nigeria; search summaries): hire a DJ who knows Nigerian music, ask whether they accommodate cultural and traditional requests, ask to see the playlist and whether they can play a few songs you expect, and tell them what you do not want. USED as "wedding planners say the same thing: tell your DJ your songs, and ask if he can play them, before the day."
