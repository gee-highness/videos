# es04 research: Chief Pete Edochie (1947 to 2026), a tribute

Researched 26 Nov 2026 from the user's Wikipedia PDF plus Vanguard, AllAfrica, The Star (BBC copy), Pulse and Particle. Labels: FACT (outlet states it) / CLAIM (a named person said it) / UNVERIFIED.
Tone: solemn, warm, a tribute. No photos or AI faces of him or his family: symbols, places and silhouettes only (red chief's cap, iroko tree, microphone, old TV, medal, empty armchair).

## Death
- FACT: died Wednesday 25 Nov 2026, aged 79, in Enugu, Enugu State. [WIKI][STAR][VAN][PULSE]
- FACT: 12:30 a.m. per the family statement. [STAR][VAN] (not used in the video)
- FACT: cause prostate cancer after a prolonged battle (The Star and Wikipedia say so; Vanguard says "reportedly"). Not said in the video.
- FACT: announced by the family in a statement headed "An Iroko Has Fallen", signed by eldest son Chief Leo Pete Edochie, posted on Instagram. [VAN][AFR][PULSE]
- FACT: funeral arrangements to be announced later; no date or venue. [AFR][VAN][PULSE]
- FACT: in Feb 2026 a death rumour was denied ("alive and kicking", Vanguard 10 Feb 2026); this time the family confirmed it. [PULSE][VAN]

## Life
- FACT: born 7 March 1947 in Zaria; Igbo, from Akamnato, Ifite Nteje, Anambra State. Full name Peter Uwadiegwu Ogugua Edochie. [WIKI][PULSE][STAR]
- FACT: St Patrick's and St James Primary, Zaria; St John's College, Kaduna; School of Journalism and Television in England. [WIKI][PULSE][STAR]
- FACT (Wikipedia): at 15, in 1962, he dramatised scenes of "The Merchant of Venice" at St John's College.
- FACT: 1967 joined the Eastern Nigeria broadcasting service (ENBS, later Anambra Broadcasting Service); rose to Director of Programmes and Deputy Managing Director. (Name differs: Wikipedia "Service", Pulse "Corporation": the video says "the broadcasting service in the East".) [WIKI][PULSE][VAN]
- FACT (Wikipedia): he left broadcasting after the government politicised the FM station, and moved into films. Not used.
- FACT: practising Catholic. [WIKI]

## Roles
- FACT: Okonkwo in the NTA television adaptation of Chinua Achebe's "Things Fall Apart", 1987 (Wikipedia); the BBC, partnered with NTA, sent a delegation to interview him about the portrayal. Outlets: the role that made him a household name. [WIKI][PULSE][VAN]
- FACT: other titles named: Rituals (1997), Evil Men (1998), Igodo (1999), Chain Reaction (1999), Across the Niger (2004), Egg of Life (2003), Billionaire's Club (2003), Lionheart (2018, as Chief Ernest Obiagu). [WIKI][PULSE][VAN]
- FACT (Pulse): Lionheart was Nigeria's first Netflix original film, directed by Genevieve Nnaji.
- FACT (Vanguard, AllAfrica): "over 200 screen productions" (the count is the outlets').
- FACT (Vanguard): often cast as traditional rulers, wealthy businessmen, fathers and family patriarchs.
- Wikipedia's filmography has duplicates and gaps: use only the titles above.

## Voice and inspiration
- FACT: deep, unhurried voice; frequent proverbs; commanding screen presence. [VAN][PULSE][AFR]
- CLAIM (Gov. Chukwuma Soludo): "the very sound of Igbo tradition on screen"; "Through his craft, he taught generations the value of dignity, courage, family and the wisdom of our elders." [VAN]
- CLAIM (Funke Akindele, paraphrased by Vanguard): worked with him on Egg of Life; he encouraged her; she called him an inspiration.
- CLAIM (Actors Guild of Nigeria president Abubakar Yakubu, paraphrased): a father figure; praised his discipline and dedication.
- CLAIM (President Tinubu): "Pete Edochie lived well. He played his part and exited gracefully." "Truly one of our finest." [VAN][PULSE]
- CLAIM (Pat Utomi): "An iroko has fallen; Nigeria has lost a voice that time will never silence." (Tola Adeniyi: "giant iroko of the theatre world".) [VAN]
- UNVERIFIED: any quote from Edochie himself about acting or advice to young people: none in the sources.

## Honours
- FACT: Member of the Order of the Niger (MON), 2003, by President Obasanjo. [WIKI][PULSE][VAN]
- FACT: AMVCA Industry Merit Award (Africa Magic), no year. FACT: Lifetime Achievement Award, Africa Film Academy, no year. FACT: African Film Legend award at TAFF, 2020. [WIKI][PULSE][VAN]

## Family left behind
- FACT: wife Barrister Josephine Edochie (Pulse: married 1969, one source); six children. Named: Leo Pete Edochie (eldest son), Yul Edochie and Linc Edochie (actors). Also survived by siblings and extended family. [WIKI][PULSE][VAN][STAR]
- UNVERIFIED: the other three children's names, grandchildren.
- FACT: he survived a kidnapping in 2009 and was released unharmed. [WIKI][PULSE] (Not used: a tribute need not retell it.)
