# The news
[img] illustration: a tall iroko tree at dusk, a red chief's cap resting on a wooden stool beneath it {move:in}
[host full solemn]
[music calm]
Nigeria has lost a giant. Chief Pete Edochie, the man who played Okonkwo, died on Wednesday, the twenty-fifth of November. He was seventy-nine.

# Where it began
[img] illustration: a school stage with a red curtain, one warm spotlight and a wooden stool {move:in}
He was born in Zaria in nineteen forty-seven.

[img same] {move:out}
At fifteen, he was already on a school stage, acting scenes from The Merchant of Venice.

[img] illustration: a vintage radio microphone on a desk, a warm glow, a reel-to-reel recorder {move:panR}
His first career was broadcasting. In nineteen sixty-seven he joined the broadcasting service in the East, and rose to Director of Programmes.

# Okonkwo
[img] illustration: an old television set glowing in a dark room, a village compound under a moon on its screen {move:in}
[music drama]
Then, in nineteen eighty-seven, came Okonkwo, in the television adaptation of Chinua Achebe's Things Fall Apart.

[img same] {move:out}
It made him a household name. The BBC even sent a team to interview him.

[img] illustration: a film clapperboard and two reels of film on a dark table, gold light {move:out}
[music calm]
By one count, he went on to more than two hundred screen productions.

[img same] {move:in}
Rituals. Igodo. Evil Men. And in twenty eighteen, Lionheart, Nigeria's first Netflix original film.

[img] illustration: a gold sound wave glowing over a woven Igbo mat, a kola nut and a calabash {move:in}
That deep, unhurried voice, and a proverb for every moment, became what Governor Soludo called, the very sound of Igbo tradition on screen.

# Honours
[img] illustration: a green and white ribbon medal on a cushion beside a small gold trophy {move:in}
In two thousand and three he was made a Member of the Order of the Niger.

[img same] {move:out}
In twenty twenty, he received the African Film Legend award.

# The inspiration
[img] illustration: silhouettes of a family sitting together in a living room, watching a glowing television {move:in}
[host solemn]
Funke Akindele, who worked with him, called him an inspiration. The Actors Guild called him a father figure.

[img] illustration: a young person's silhouette at a window with a notebook, an old television glowing behind {move:out}
He showed that a calm voice and a good story can carry a whole culture.

# Who he leaves behind
[img] illustration: an empty armchair beside a lamp, a red cap on its arm, a framed picture turned toward the wall {move:in}
[music calm]
He leaves behind his wife, Barrister Josephine Edochie, and six children, including the actors Yul and Linc Edochie.

[img same] {move:out}
His eldest son, Leo, announced it in a statement of four words: An Iroko Has Fallen.

[img] illustration: a lone iroko tree under stars, fireflies and a soft red glow on the horizon {move:in}
President Tinubu said it simply: Pete Edochie lived well.

[img same] {move:in}
[host full solemn]
Funeral details will be announced later. Rest well, Chief. And thank you for the stories.
